How to appeal a benefit decision

If you think a decision about your Universal Credit, PIP, ESA or another benefit is wrong, you can challenge it. It is free. There are two stages: first you ask DWP to look again (mandatory reconsideration), then, if you still disagree, you appeal to an independent tribunal.

Stage 1: mandatory reconsideration

Before you can appeal you usually have to ask for a mandatory reconsideration. Your decision letter will say if you can skip this step.

  • Deadline: usually within one month of the date on your decision letter. If you are late, explain why — it may still be accepted.
  • How: for Universal Credit, through your journal, by phone on 0800 328 5644, or by letter. For other benefits, contact the office that made the decision (its details are on your letter) or use the form on GOV.UK.
  • What to include: the date of the decision, your name, address, date of birth and National Insurance number, what you think is wrong and why, plus any new evidence.

You will get a "mandatory reconsideration notice" telling you whether the decision has changed. Keep it — you need it to appeal.

Stage 2: appeal to the tribunal

Appeals are decided by the Social Security and Child Support Tribunal, supported by HM Courts and Tribunals Service (HMCTS). The tribunal is independent of DWP and listens to both sides.

  • Deadline: within one month of your mandatory reconsideration notice. Later appeals need an explanation and may not be accepted.
  • Online: appeal on GOV.UK. You will need your National Insurance number, your mandatory reconsideration notice and details of anyone helping you.
  • By post: use form SSCS1 (for most benefit decisions).
  • Cost: free.

You choose whether to attend a hearing to explain your case. If you do not attend, the tribunal decides on your form and evidence. After you submit, you can track your appeal and add evidence online.

Building a strong case

  • Read the decision letter and work out exactly which points you disagree with.
  • For PIP and work capability decisions, compare your daily life with each activity or descriptor and give real examples.
  • Get supporting letters from your GP, consultant, support worker or carers.
  • Keep copies of everything and note the dates you sent things.

Getting free help

You can appoint a representative — a friend, family member or adviser — to help or act for you. Free advice is available from Citizens Advice, Advicenow and local advisers through Advicelocal. If you are struggling with the online form, the GOV.UK appeal service has web chat. See also our extra support page.

Complaints are different

An appeal is about whether a decision is right. If your problem is how you were treated — delays, rudeness or lost paperwork — that is a complaint. See complaints and appeals.

Northern Ireland and decisions by Social Security Scotland have their own processes.

Frequently asked questions

How long do I have to ask for a mandatory reconsideration?

Usually one month from the date on your decision letter.

How long do I have to appeal to the tribunal?

One month from the date of your mandatory reconsideration notice.

Does it cost anything to appeal?

No, appealing a benefit decision is free.

Which form do I use to appeal by post?

Form SSCS1 for most benefit decisions.
