Universal Credit journal and account help

Your Universal Credit online account is where most of your claim happens. It is usually the quickest way to get answers without waiting on the phone. This guide covers signing in, what you can do in your account, using the journal well and reporting changes. We are an independent guide and cannot see or access anyone's account.

What you can do in your account

  • See your statement and when your next payment will be
  • Read and agree your claimant commitment
  • Check your to-do list, such as appointments your work coach has booked
  • Report a change of circumstances
  • Apply for an advance on your first payment
  • Send messages to your work coach or case manager through your journal

Sign in through the official page: Sign in to your Universal Credit account on GOV.UK. A Welsh-language version is available.

Can't sign in? Forgotten username or password

You sign in with the username and password you created when you applied. If you are not sure of them, use the reminder option on the official sign-in page to get a reminder of your details. A few things that often help:

  • Make sure you are on the real GOV.UK sign-in page (the web address should be on gov.uk). Never type your details into a page you reached from an advert or text message link you were not expecting.
  • Check for typing errors, caps lock and autofill filling in an old password.
  • If you changed your phone number or email, the reminder may go to the old one — call the helpline on 0800 328 5644 (Welsh 0800 328 1744) for help.
  • Try a different browser, or clear your browser's cache, if the page will not load.

Never share your password with anyone, including people offering to "help" with your claim. Genuine advisers, such as Help to Claim, will not need it.

Using your journal well

The journal is a message thread between you and the Universal Credit team. It is useful because everything is in writing.

  • Be specific. Say what you need and include dates and amounts: "My payment on 14 November was £120 lower than last month. Please can you explain the deduction?"
  • One topic per message makes it easier to answer and to find later.
  • Allow a few days for a reply. You will usually get a text or email when your journal or to-do list is updated, so make sure your contact details are up to date.
  • Keep copies. Screenshots of important messages are helpful if you later need to challenge a decision or complain.
  • Urgent? If you have no money for food, heating or rent, or your situation has suddenly changed, call the helpline as well as messaging.

Reporting a change of circumstances

You need to report changes as soon as they happen, by signing in to your account. A change can increase or reduce your payment, and it can affect the whole assessment period it falls in — not just from the day you report it. Reporting late can mean you are underpaid, or overpaid and have to pay it back. Giving wrong information or not reporting a change can lead to a penalty or court action.

Common changes include:

  • Starting or finishing a job (if you are self-employed, changes in your earnings)
  • Having a baby, or a partner moving in or out
  • Moving home, or your rent going up or down
  • Changing your bank details, phone number or email
  • Starting to care for a child or disabled person
  • Changes to your health, or becoming too ill to work or meet your work coach
  • Changes to savings or investments
  • Going abroad for any length of time
  • A change to your immigration status

Most employers report your earnings for you, but you should still tell Universal Credit when you start a new job.

Frequently asked questions

How do I reset my Universal Credit password?

Go to the official sign-in page on GOV.UK and use the reminder option for your username or password. If that does not work, call the helpline on 0800 328 5644.

How long does Universal Credit take to reply to a journal message?

It can take a few days. You will usually get a text or email when there is a reply.

How do I report a change of circumstances?

Sign in to your Universal Credit account and report it as soon as it happens.

Can someone else use my account for me?

You should never share your password. A trusted person or adviser can help you, but you should sign in yourself.
