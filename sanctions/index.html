Universal Credit sanctions explained

A sanction is a cut to your Universal Credit when DWP decides you did not do something in your claimant commitment without a good reason — for example missing an appointment or not applying for a job you were told to apply for. This guide explains how sanctions work, how much they cost, and what you can do.

Can't make an appointment or activity? Tell DWP before it happens if you can — add a note to your journal or use the contact details your work coach gave you. If they accept you had a good reason, your payment is not reduced.

Good reasons DWP may accept

Examples include a hospital appointment at the same time as a meeting, being unexpectedly ill, or a domestic emergency that stops you attending an interview. Always explain and, where you can, send evidence.

How much a sanction takes

A sanction reduces your standard allowance — by 100% for each day it lasts, or by 40% if you are 16 or 17 or your only requirement is to attend work-focused appointments. Extra amounts for children and housing costs are still paid.

You may have less taken off in some situations, such as caring for young children or disabled people, being pregnant with your baby due within 11 weeks, or having had a baby in the last 15 weeks. You will not have two sanctions at once, but they can run back-to-back. A sanction may also affect other help, such as help with NHS costs.

The four sanction levels

Lowest

If your only requirement is to attend appointments about work and you miss one without good reason. It lasts until the day before you contact DWP to rearrange — so get in touch quickly.

Low (the most common)

For things like missing a work-focused interview, not giving evidence you were asked for, or not going on a recommended training course. It lasts until you do the activity, plus extra days: usually 7 for a first sanction in a year, rising to 14 and then 28 for repeat sanctions.

Medium

For not doing enough to look for work or not being available for work. 28 days for the first in a 365-day period; up to 91 days for a repeat (7 and 14 days if you are 16 or 17).

High

For things like turning down a job offer, or leaving a job or losing pay by choice or through misconduct, without good reason. 91 days for the first in a year, up to 182 days for a repeat (14 and 28 days if you are 16 or 17).

What you will be told

You will get a journal message or letter saying how much your payment is reduced by, how long the sanction may last or what you must do to end it, and what you failed to do.

If you can't afford essentials

If a sanction has cut your payment, you may be able to get a recoverable hardship payment for food, heating, hygiene items or housing costs not covered by Universal Credit. You must have been sanctioned at 100% of your standard allowance (50% or more for a couple), and have met your work requirements in the seven days before applying. You repay it from future payments once the sanction ends. Full details: advances and hardship payments.

Challenging a sanction

If you think the sanction is wrong, or you have new evidence, ask for a mandatory reconsideration — usually within one month — and then appeal to the tribunal if needed. See how to appeal.

Avoiding sanctions

  • Read your claimant commitment and ask about anything you do not understand.
  • Keep a record of your job search and activities.
  • Report changes in your circumstances so your commitment stays realistic.
  • Check your journal regularly and turn on text or email alerts.

Need help? Universal Credit helpline 0800 328 5644, Monday to Friday, 8am to 6pm (free). For independent advice, see extra support.

Frequently asked questions

How much is a Universal Credit sanction per day?

At the 100% rate: £11.10 single under 25, £13.90 single 25 or over, £8.60 or £10.90 per sanctioned person in a joint claim.

Are child and housing amounts sanctioned?

No. Sanctions reduce the standard allowance; extra amounts for children and housing are still paid.

How long does a low level sanction last?

Until you do the activity, plus usually 7 extra days for a first sanction, then 14 or 28 for repeats.

Can I get money while sanctioned?

You may be able to get a recoverable hardship payment for essentials, which you repay later.
