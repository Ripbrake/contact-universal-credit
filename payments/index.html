Universal Credit payments, advances and missing payments

Universal Credit is usually paid once a month, straight into your bank, building society or credit union account. This guide explains when you get paid, how advances work, why payments can be lower than expected, and who to contact if a payment is missing. It is an independent guide — for your own figures, check the statement in your online account.

When you get paid

  • Your first payment usually takes around five weeks.
  • Universal Credit is worked out over a monthly assessment period, which starts on the day you claim. You are usually paid about seven days after each assessment period ends.
  • After that, you are paid on the same date each month. For example, if you claim on 10 September, your first assessment period runs to 9 October and you would usually be paid around 17 October, then on the 17th of each month.
  • If your payment date falls on a weekend or bank holiday, you are paid on the working day before.
  • Your statement in your online account shows how much you will get.
  • If you live with a partner and you both claim, you get one payment for your household.

Scotland: once or twice a month

If you live in Scotland you can choose to be paid once or twice a month. New claimants are asked after their first payment; if you already claim and have not been asked, speak to your work coach.

Advances: help before your first payment

If you cannot manage while you wait for your first payment, you can ask for an advance. The most you can get is the amount of your first estimated payment. You can apply through your journal, by asking your work coach or jobcentre, or by calling the helpline on 0800 328 5644. You will need to explain why you need it, confirm your identity, give bank details and tell them about any money or savings you have. You usually find out the same day.

An advance is a loan, not extra money. You usually pay it back over up to 24 months, starting from your first payment, so your monthly payments will be lower. Only borrow what you need.

Budgeting Advance for one-off costs

If you already get Universal Credit and need help with a one-off cost — such as a cooker, washing machine, furniture or costs of getting a job — you might get a Budgeting Advance. The smallest amount is £100, and the most is £348 if you are single, £464 for a couple, or £812 if you have children. Usually you need to have been getting Universal Credit, Employment and Support Allowance or Pension Credit for six months or more (unless it is to help you start or stay in work), and you will not qualify if you earned more than £2,600 (£3,600 for couples) in the last six months or still owe a previous Budgeting Advance. It cannot be used for ongoing costs like food, bills, rent or paying off debts.

Why your payment might be lower

Money can be taken off ("deductions") if, for example:

  • you are repaying an advance
  • the benefit cap applies to you
  • you were overpaid benefits in the past
  • you owe money for things like Council Tax, court fines, gas or electricity

Changes in your earnings or circumstances during the assessment period can also change the amount. Your statement shows how your payment was worked out. If you cannot afford deductions, contact Universal Credit through your journal or the helpline to discuss it, and consider free debt advice (see extra support).

If your payment is missing

  1. Check your statement and payment date in your online account — remember the weekend and bank holiday rule.
  2. Check with your bank that the account details on your claim are correct and up to date.
  3. Look for journal messages or to-do items that might need action, such as an appointment you were asked to attend.
  4. Contact Universal Credit through your journal, or call 0800 328 5644 (Welsh 0800 328 1744), Monday to Friday 8am to 6pm. If you have no money for essentials, call rather than wait.

If your payments have been stopped or reduced, you may be able to ask for a hardship payment.

Changing how you are paid

If you are struggling, you can ask for an alternative payment arrangement, such as having your housing costs paid straight to your landlord, or more frequent payments. These are more likely if you have rent arrears or other difficulties. Ask through your journal or the helpline after you have claimed.

Frequently asked questions

When will I get my first Universal Credit payment?

It usually takes around five weeks. You can ask for an advance if you need money before then.

What happens if my payment date is a bank holiday?

You will be paid on the working day before.

How much is a Budgeting Advance?

From £100 up to £348 if single, £464 for a couple or £812 if you have children, depending on eligibility.

Who do I call about a missing Universal Credit payment?

Check your statement first, then message through your journal or call 0800 328 5644 (Monday to Friday, 8am to 6pm).
